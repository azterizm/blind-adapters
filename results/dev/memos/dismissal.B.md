# dismissal, arm B: uk_unfair_dismissal
Status: ANSWERED (every step computed or grounded)

## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 95(1)(a), the employee is dismissed if the contract is terminated by the employer. The employer terminated the contract by informing the employee of dismissal with immediate effect. Section 95(1)(c) also applies if the employee terminates the contract due to the employer's conduct, but in this case, the employer terminated the contract. The effective date of termination under section 97(1)(a) is the date the notice expires, which in this case is the date of immediate effect, confirming the dismissal.
  > "On 12 March 2026 her manager, Dave Pritchard, telephoned her and told her she was dismissed with immediate effect for gross misconduct"
  > "She received a short dismissal letter dated 13 March 2026"
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s95, uk/ukpga/1996/18/s97

## automatic_unfair_reason: NO [grounded]
Section 108(3) does not apply because the dismissal was not due to a reason listed in sections 99, 100, or 103A. The dismissal was for gross misconduct related to unauthorized absence, which is not an automatically unfair reason under those sections. The evidence does not indicate that the dismissal was for pregnancy, maternity, health and safety, or protected disclosure.
  > "On 12 March 2026 her manager, Dave Pritchard, telephoned her and told her she was dismissed with immediate effect for gross misconduct, namely three days' unauthorised absence from 16 to 18 February 2026."

## fairness: YES [grounded]
Under section 98(1) of the Employment Rights Act 1996, the employer must show a fair reason for dismissal. The employer failed to provide any investigation or evidence of the alleged misconduct, and the dismissal was for gross misconduct without proper procedure. Section 98(4) requires the employer to act reasonably in treating the reason as sufficient, which was not demonstrated here.
