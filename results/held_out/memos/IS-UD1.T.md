## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 95(1)(a), the employee is dismissed by the employer if the contract is terminated by the employer, whether with or without notice. In this case, the employer gave notice of termination on 4 September 2026, and the notice expired on 2 October 2026, which is the effective date of termination. The employee was dismissed by the employer as the contract was terminated by the employer.

## automatic_unfair_reason: NO [grounded]
Section 108(3) disapplies the two-year qualifying period only if the dismissal falls under specific statutory exceptions, such as those in sections 99, 100, or 103A. In this case, the dismissal was for alleged misconduct related to expenses, which does not fall under any of these statutory exceptions. Therefore, the two-year qualifying period applies.
  > "On 4 September 2026 she was given four weeks' notice of dismissal for alleged misconduct after a dispute about expenses, and her employment ended when that notice expired on 2 October 2026."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s108

## fairness: UNCLEAR [unclear]
Under section 98(1), the employer must establish the reason for the dismissal and demonstrate that it falls within a statutory category or is a substantial reason. The employer dismissed Ms Nair for alleged misconduct, which falls under 'conduct' (section 98(2)(b)). However, the employer did not conduct an investigation or disciplinary hearing, which may affect the reasonableness of the dismissal under section 98(4). The tribunal would need to assess whether the employer acted reasonably in treating the misconduct as sufficient grounds for dismissal, considering the employer's size and administrative resources.
  > "There was no investigation and no disciplinary hearing"
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s98
