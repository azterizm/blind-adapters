# NV-CD1, arm TEACHER: uk_unfair_dismissal

## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 95(1)(c) of the Employment Rights Act 1996, an employee is dismissed where they terminate the contract (with or without notice) in circumstances in which they are entitled to terminate it without notice by reason of the employer's conduct. The employer committed a repudiatory breach by unilaterally demoting Ms Dunmore to a junior role not permitted by her contract, removing her team, and publicly accusing her of dishonesty without reason. Ms Dunmore resigned without notice on 4 September 2026 because of this conduct, which under section 97(1)(b) constitutes the effective date of termination taking effect on that date.

  > "Her contract does not allow the employer to change her role and she did not agree to the move."
  > "She resigned without notice on 4 September 2026 because of the demotion and the accusation."

## automatic_unfair_reason: NO [grounded]
Under sections 99, 100, and 103A of the Employment Rights Act 1996, a dismissal is automatically unfair if the reason or principal reason relates to statutory family leave, health and safety activities, or a protected disclosure. Ms Dunmore resigned because she was unilaterally demoted, had her team removed, and was publicly accused of dishonesty. There are no facts indicating that her dismissal was connected in any way to family leave, health and safety matters, or making a protected disclosure.

## fairness: YES [grounded]
Under section 98(1), the burden is on the employer to show a potentially fair statutory reason for dismissal under section 98(2) or another substantial reason. Here, the employer demoted the client without contractual authority, accused her of dishonesty without giving any reason, and failed to respond to her written complaint. Consequently, the employer cannot demonstrate a fair statutory reason under section 98(1)–(2) and, by failing to investigate, provide reasons, or respond to her grievance, acted unreasonably under section 98(4).
  > "On 1 July 2026 the managing director moved her to a junior negotiator role, took her team away from her and, at a staff meeting, accused her of dishonesty without giving any reason."
  > "Her contract does not allow the employer to change her role and she did not agree to the move."
  > "She complained in writing and received no reply."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s98
