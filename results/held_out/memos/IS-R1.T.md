# IS-R1, arm T: uk_redundancy_payment
Status: ANSWERED (every step computed or grounded)

## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 136(1)(a), an employee is dismissed if the employer terminates the contract. In this case, the employer gave notice of the factory closure, which terminated the contract. Section 136(5) also states that an act of the employer, such as closure, operates to terminate the contract by operation of law. Therefore, the employee was dismissed by the employer within the statutory definition.
  > "On 1 September 2026 she was given written notice that the company was closing its Coventry factory and that her employment would end on 30 September 2026; it ended on that date."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s136

## by_reason_of_redundancy: YES [grounded]
The dismissal is wholly or mainly attributable to the employer ceasing business, as the company closed its Coventry factory, and the employee's employment ended as a result. The statutory test in section 139(1)(a) is satisfied as the employer ceased to carry on the business in the place where the employee was employed.
  > "On 1 September 2026 she was given written notice that the company was closing its Coventry factory and that her employment would end on 30 September 2026; it ended on that date."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s139

## suitable_alternative: YES [grounded]
Under section 141, the employee is not entitled to a redundancy payment if she unreasonably refuses an offer of suitable employment. In this case, the employee was not offered any other job, so there was no offer to renew the contract or re-engage her. Therefore, the employee retains the right to a redundancy payment.
  > "She was not offered any other job."
  > "Her employment would end on 30 September 2026."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s141
