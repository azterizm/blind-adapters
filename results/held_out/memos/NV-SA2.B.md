## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 136(1)(a), an employee is dismissed by the employer if the contract is terminated by the employer. In this case, the employer gave written notice that the branch would close and the employee's employment would end on 30 September 2026. This constitutes the employer terminating the contract, which meets the statutory definition of dismissal.
  > "On 1 September 2026 the company gave her written notice that the Plymouth branch would close and that her employment would end on 30 September 2026."
  > "Her employment ended on 30 September 2026."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s136

## by_reason_of_redundancy: YES [grounded]
The dismissal is wholly or mainly attributable to the employer ceasing business as the Plymouth branch was closed, which falls under paragraph (a)(i) of subsection (1). The closure of the branch directly led to the employee's redundancy.
  > "On 1 September 2026 the company gave her written notice that the Plymouth branch would close and that her employment would end on 30 September 2026."
  > "Her employment ended on 30 September 2026."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s139

## suitable_alternative: YES [grounded]
Section 141(2) states that the employee is not entitled to a redundancy payment if they unreasonably refuse an offer. In this case, the employer offered a junior booking clerk position at a Leeds office, which is significantly different from the previous contract. The employee refused the offer, which was not suitable due to the distance and pay reduction, and the offer was not suitable in relation to her. Therefore, the employee is entitled to a redundancy payment.
  > "Before her employment ended it offered her a junior booking clerk job at its Leeds office, about 300 miles away, starting on 5 October 2026, at a salary a little over half of her current salary."
  > "She refused the offer on 8 September 2026."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s141
