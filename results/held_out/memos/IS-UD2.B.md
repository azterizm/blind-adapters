# IS-UD2, arm B: uk_unfair_dismissal
Status: ANSWERED (every step computed or grounded)

## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 95(1)(a), an employee is dismissed if the contract is terminated by the employer. The employee was dismissed with immediate effect, indicating the contract was terminated by the employer. Section 95(1)(c) also applies if the employee terminates the contract due to the employer's conduct, but in this case, the employer terminated the contract. The effective date of termination under section 97(1)(a) is the date the notice expires, but since no notice was given, the effective date is the date of termination, which is 19 June 2026.
  > "He was dismissed with immediate effect on 19 June 2026 after his manager said the kitchen had failed a hygiene inspection."
  > "He was not given notice or pay in lieu of notice."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s95, uk/ukpga/1996/18/s97

## automatic_unfair_reason: NO [grounded]
Section 108(3) of the Employment Rights Act 1996 disapplies the two-year qualifying period if the dismissal is due to a reason specified in sections 99, 100, or 103A. However, the facts do not indicate that the dismissal was for a reason covered by these sections. The dismissal was due to a hygiene inspection failure, which does not fall under the protected categories outlined in sections 99, 100, or 103A. Therefore, the two-year qualifying period would apply.

## fairness: YES [grounded]
Under section 98(1) of the Employment Rights Act 1996, the employer must show a fair reason for dismissal. The employer's reason for dismissal was a hygiene inspection failure, which may fall under 'conduct of the employee' (section 98(2)(b)). However, the employer must also demonstrate that it was reasonable to treat this as a sufficient reason (section 98(4)). The failure to provide notice or pay in lieu of notice may also impact the fairness of the dismissal.
  > "He was not given notice or pay in lieu of notice."
  authority: uk/ukpga/1996/18/s98
