## dismissed: YES [grounded]
Under section 136(1)(a), an employee is dismissed by the employer if the contract is terminated by the employer, whether with or without notice. In this case, the employer informed Gareth Pryce that his job would end on 28 August 2026, which constitutes termination of the contract by the employer. Additionally, the employer's notice of redundancy and the subsequent refusal of a new role confirm the termination of the contract, meeting the statutory definition of dismissal under section 136.

## by_reason_of_redundancy: YES [grounded]
The dismissal is wholly or mainly attributable to the employer's decision to reduce the number of claims handlers from ten to six, which diminishes the requirements for employees to carry out work of a particular kind. This meets the statutory test under section 139(1)(b) of the Employment Rights Act 1996.

## suitable_alternative: YES [grounded]
Under section 141, the employee is not entitled to a redundancy payment if he unreasonably refuses an offer of suitable employment. In this case, the employee was offered a position in the same office with the same pay, hours, and other terms, which constitutes suitable employment. However, the employee refused the offer, and there is no indication that the offer was unsuitable. Therefore, the employee is not entitled to a redundancy payment, but the question asks if the employee keeps the right to a redundancy payment, which would be 'no' if the employee unreasonably refused the offer. However, the question is phrased as a yes/no based on the conditions outlined, and the answer is 'yes' if the conditions for the employee to keep the right are met, which they are in this case.
